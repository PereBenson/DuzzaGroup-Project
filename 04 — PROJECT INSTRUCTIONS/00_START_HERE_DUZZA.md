# DUZZA GROUP — CREATIVE DIRECTION AND DESIGN REQUIREMENTS

## 1. The design problem

DUZZA GROUP brings together two active businesses with very different needs:

- Projects needs to communicate technical capability, reliability and a clear route to an enquiry.
- Branding needs to demonstrate creative quality through its own visual presentation and portfolio.

The parent website must connect them without making them look like the same business.

The website should feel contemporary, confident and well considered. It should not look like a conventional contractor template, a generic conglomerate website or a generic digital-agency template.

## 2. What the competitive research tells us

### Projects

The research found that Nigerian engineering and marine firms commonly use service lists, corporate credibility statements, project imagery, client references and certifications.

Useful patterns to investigate:
- AMSL: broad service organisation and maritime positioning.
- GOSL: clear service groups, project references and fleet/equipment presentation.
- Niger Global Engineering: strong hero imagery, capability hierarchy and motion.
- Batoil: visible equipment-rental service, project filtering and testimonials.
- Oceanquip: equipment catalogue, product detail and hire-request experience.

Design implications:
- A visitor should quickly understand what DUZZA Projects does.
- Separate services from equipment hire, but make both easy to find.
- Give each equipment item a useful detail view and enquiry route once the real inventory is supplied.
- Use real project evidence, technical imagery, maps, survey outputs and equipment photographs where approved.
- Put a clear enquiry action near the relevant service or equipment.
- Do not copy a large, complicated service mega-menu just because a competitor has one.

The research found limited independent customer-review evidence for many firms. Do not claim that a competitor has poor customer service or a bad website unless there is reliable evidence for that specific claim.

### Branding

The research shows a gap between agencies that describe broad capabilities and agencies that convincingly show their creative work.

Useful patterns to investigate:
- Wild Fusion: concise positioning, client/work visibility and a modern visual structure.
- Ogilvy and Insight Redefini: established network positioning and breadth, but their websites are not necessarily the best portfolio-design models.
- Pentagram: strong work-led presentation and visual craft.
- Other international studios in the research: use them to assess image scale, case-study storytelling, typography and interaction.

Design implications:
- Lead with strong work, not a long agency introduction.
- Use large image-led project cards.
- Give selected projects enough room to show the quality of the work.
- Separate executive branding, corporate/digital branding, creative media and other verified services.
- Explain what DUZZA actually delivers in plain English.
- Show process and outcomes only when the business can substantiate them.
- Do not invent client names, campaign results or international markets.

## 3. User-approved preferences

These are the preferences to preserve during exploration:

- Sharp-edged, rectangular cards rather than rounded cards.
- Large, image-led project and creative cards.
- Clean, airy layouts with generous whitespace.
- A full-width image treatment in the homepage hero.
- Concise, punchy, businesslike copy.
- Purposeful animation and interaction.
- Mobile-first design, not a desktop design reduced in size.
- Separate Businesses and Work navigation.
- Separate dropdown/mega-menu experiences for those two navigation areas.
- Fashion and Food visible as future businesses, clearly labelled Coming Soon.
- Footer space for Instagram, LinkedIn and X.
- Email link should use the correct `mailto:` destination only after it is confirmed.

## 4. Reference-image review

Inspect the images in `Reference Images/` individually.

Pay particular attention to the references the owner has identified:

- Greenaway Architectural — especially its project/works wall and grid.
- NORDA — especially the hero.
- HENGE — especially the first image and hero treatment.
- Raben Rifaie / RabenRifaie — overall visual presentation.

For each reference, record:
1. What the owner appears to like.
2. The layout or interaction pattern being referenced.
3. What can transfer to DUZZA.
4. What should not be copied.
5. How it behaves on mobile, if the reference can be inspected.

These are visual references, not necessarily business competitors.

## 5. Palette exploration — no approved palette yet

The earlier charcoal, off-white and lime proposal was not approved and was not validated through sufficient comparison. Do not treat it as the design system.

Present at least three genuinely different colour directions. Compare each direction using the same sample layouts:
- Group homepage.
- Projects capability or equipment section.
- Branding portfolio section.
- A future Fashion/Food teaser.

Show how each palette behaves with photography, text, links, buttons, backgrounds and cards. Check text contrast and accessibility. Explain the practical strengths and weaknesses of each direction.

Do not select a palette on the basis of personal preference alone. Make the comparison visible so the owner can decide.

## 6. Typography exploration

The previously discussed Space Grotesk, Inter and IBM Plex Mono direction is a candidate, not a final decision.

Compare it with at least one credible alternative pairing. Show real sample headings, body text, navigation and technical labels. Assess readability on mobile, personality, suitability for both technical and creative businesses, and availability/performance.

Do not use too many font families. The final system should be simple and consistent.

## 7. Layout and interaction requirements

- Use a consistent grid and spacing system.
- Keep cards rectangular and sharp-edged.
- Do not make every section a card grid. Use full-width image sections, editorial layouts and varied compositions where they improve the experience.
- Use hover effects only where a pointer exists; provide touch equivalents.
- Keep navigation usable by keyboard and touch.
- Avoid scroll hijacking and motion that blocks access to content.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Use optimised responsive images and video.
- Make the mobile navigation easy to understand, with expandable Businesses and Work groups.
- Keep forms short and relevant to the enquiry.
- Make the enquiry route obvious on Projects and Branding pages.

## 8. Required design review output

Before implementation, present:
1. Three visual direction options with reasons.
2. Three palette comparisons applied to the same sample layouts.
3. Typography comparison.
4. Proposed homepage composition.
5. Projects page composition.
6. Branding page composition.

The owner must approve the direction before production implementation.
